SCIENCE & THERAPEUTICS

Current research and treatment landscape

Species matters: licensed EBOV tools should not be assumed effective for Bundibugyo, Sudan or other orthoebolaviruses.

Home

Supportive care

Early intensive supportive care, including oral or IV rehydration and treatment of specific symptoms, improves survival. This remains fundamental across Ebola diseases.

Inmazeb (REGN-EB3)

Three monoclonal antibodies. FDA-approved for infection caused by Orthoebolavirus zairense. Approval: 14 Oct 2020.

FDA Ebola medical countermeasures →

Ebanga (ansuvimab)

Monoclonal antibody. FDA-approved for infection caused by Orthoebolavirus zairense. Approval: 21 Dec 2020. Efficacy is not established for other Orthoebolavirus species.

FDA approval information →

Ervebo vaccine

Licensed and WHO-prequalified for Ebola virus disease caused by EBOV. For the 2026 Bundibugyo outbreak, WHO says human protective efficacy is not established and recommends use within research protocols.

WHO vaccine Q&A →

Bundibugyo research

WHO reported a Phase 3 / ring-vaccination research response and development priorities for BDBV-specific vaccines. Research aims to establish whether existing or candidate vaccines can protect humans.

WHO 2026 emergency vaccine guidance →

PARTNERS therapeutic trial

WHO reported that the PARTNERS clinical trial began enrollment in July 2026 to evaluate treatments for Bundibugyo virus disease in DRC clinical-management facilities.
